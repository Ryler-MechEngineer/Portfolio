# AI-Powered Vending Machine Robot

**Role:** Engineering Team Lead, Rowdy Creators, November 2025 to May 2026
**Tools:** SOLIDWORKS, 3D printing
**Focus:** Mechanical system design, drivetrain layout, integration, mentoring

## Summary

I led the mechanical side of a mobile robot meant to navigate on its own and dispense items. I was responsible for the physical system: the drivetrain, the structure, and the platform that carries the upper components. I did not own the full electronics and software stack.

## Objective

Build a mobile robot that combines a mechanical platform with electrical, sensing, and software subsystems so it can navigate and dispense items on its own.

## What I did

- Led the mechanical design and built the base CAD assembly.
- Designed the drivetrain layout, structure, and mounting platform for the upper components.
- Helped teammates who were new to engineering design develop usable parts.
- Coordinated subsystem work so the pieces would assemble into one robot.
- A teammate's public LinkedIn recommendation credits me with contributing critical components to the project.

## Challenges

- Turning the overall robot concept into a design that could be built.
- Fitting the drivetrain and the upper components into the available structure.
- Coordinating design interfaces across mechanical, electrical, and software work.
- Helping teammates who were less experienced with design make parts that worked.
- Moving from a CAD assembly to a physical build while keeping the design practical.

## What failed

PLA servo horns and other PLA components failed under load. The team moved to metal components where that made sense.

## What I took from it

Something working in CAD is not the same as something that survives real loads. PLA is good for checking geometry and fit, and parts that carry load need to be designed and made for it.
