**Role:** Mechanical Engineering Intern, Houston Energy Corridor, summer 2026 (May to August)
**Tools:** SOLIDWORKS, 3D printing
**Focus:** Fixture design, root-cause analysis, QA/QC, process standardization

## Summary

I designed a new manufacturing fixture for a process that was not working. The reported failure rate dropped from over 30% to 4% and output went up 50%. A manufacturing engineer's public LinkedIn recommendation credits my fixture and my broader process-improvement work.

## Objective

Improve an existing manufacturing process through mechanical design, process updates, and quality control, without disrupting what production needed to keep running.

## What I did

- Designed a new fixture to improve process performance, repeatability, and output.
- Helped manufacturing engineers update and upgrade existing processes.
- Supported QA and QC work, CAD design, troubleshooting, and process improvement.
- Saw how an engineering concept becomes a production-ready, standardized process.
- Had exposure to ISO 9001 and lean practices.

## Design decisions

The process did not work before I got there. I was told the engineers had already tried to fix it and could not. I treated the failures as a pattern to solve, not as one-off defects, and I focused on the workholding step.

I tried different configurations in a flexible 3D-printed material until I found a sponge-like pattern. It deforms easily, so it does not fight the part, but it still holds the part in place.
