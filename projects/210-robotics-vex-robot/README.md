# 210 Robotics: VEX U Competition Robot

**Roles:** Co-founder and Project Manager, design checker, and match day coach for one of our two robots
**Organization:** 210 Robotics, a student VEX U organization at UTSA, about 60 members
**Result:** Reached the semifinals at our first competition, October 2026 (a five-team event)
**Tools:** Gantt chart tracking, design reviews, CAD review
**Focus:** Project management, design review, drivetrain serviceability, match strategy

## Summary

I co-founded 210 Robotics because I saw a gap in advanced, hands-on robotics at UTSA. We started with about 20 interested students and about 8 committed members, and we now have about 60. We built two copies of a VEX U competition robot, which we call V1 and bot 2. My role was Project Manager. I also checked the robot's structural design, and on match days I coached one of our two robots from the sideline.

I did not design the robot alone. Much of my work was coordination: gathering project information, consolidating updates across subsystem teams, tracking progress, and supporting planning. My direct technical work was design review and a drivetrain assembly fix, plus general build labor.

## Design decisions and why I made them

**I pushed back the deadline for the first robot.** The team estimated one week to finish bot 1. I overrode that and built in extra buffer days, because I expected delays from 3D printing and from ordered parts. The buffer turned out to be necessary, and the team stayed on my pushed-back date.

**I split the match day jobs.** The driver only drives. I coach from the sideline and call moves. A driver who is focused on controlling the robot cannot also watch the whole field, so a second person who can see everything gives us the best shot at winning the match.

**I structured the team with clear owners.** Clear responsibilities, a direct reporting structure, technical team leads, updates between subsystems, and accountability for schedules. Club officers and technical leads are separate roles, so there is no overlap in who decides what. I did this because unclear ownership and poor information flow are what stall a team the most.

**I unblocked the design team.** The design team was stalled because our strategist had the best scoring plan but had not shared it. I worked out exactly what the designers needed in order to proceed, and I contacted the strategist to get it.

## What failed, and what I changed

**Reaching one drivetrain motor was slow.** Originally, getting to a single motor meant fully disassembling the drivetrain, which took about 20 minutes. I standardized all 8 shaft-to-motor configurations to one consistent orientation. Reassembly now takes under 5 minutes, and getting to a motor later is easier too.
